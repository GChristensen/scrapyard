Keep the page, not just the link.

Scrapyard is a bookmarking extension with a polished interface and almost no setup that keeps a faithful copy of the pages you save.

Add to Firefox Add to Chrome
01What it is

Links rot. Pages get edited, paywalled, moved or deleted. Scrapyard stores the whole page, or just the part you need, right next to your bookmarks, so what you saved still opens when the original doesn't.

Built as a modern alternative to the legacy ScrapBook add-on, with import for ScrapBook RDF archives.

02Features

Four things it gets right.

01

Very easy setup

Install it and start saving. No intricate configuration, cumbersome backends and configs. Use automated filesystem backend app installer in Windows.

02

A polished, familiar interface

A sidebar tree to browse and a one-click popup to capture. It stays close to the original ScrapBook, so there is little to learn.

03

Faithful capture

Complete HTML pages, page fragments and PDF documents are saved the way you saw them, layout and images included.

04

Multiple bookmark shelves

Separate bookmark trees you can group your archives into. Keep each subject on its own shelf, or filter the sidebar to see everything at once.

Choose where archives live.

Filesystem

Kept in a folder you choose. Needs the Scrapyard backend app on your computer.

Server

Kept on a server you host, on your LAN or online. A VPN is advised for the online self-hosting.

Browser storage

Kept inside the browser profile. No backend app needed, but only for simple use cases.

03Interface

Quiet on purpose.

A sidebar to browse, a popup to capture. An interface close to the original ScrapBook.

The sidebar

Browse

Shelves, folders and every saved page in one tree, with search and a shelf menu within reach.

The capture popup

Capture

Name it, pick a folder, add tags. Then choose Bookmark to save just a link, or Archive for a full copy.

04Text interface

Bookmark with a sentence.

Scrapyard also works with iShell, a keyboard-first command prompt. It follows the interface principles of Jef Raskin, author of The Humane Interface, to cut the bookmarking down to three steps: summon the prompt with Ctrl+Space, say where the page goes, press Enter.

One prompt

One place to type

There is no buttons to hunt with the mouse and no folder trees to explore. Destination folder names are autocompleted.

No modes

Nothing to switch into

The prompt appears over the page and vanishes when the command runs, so your attention never leaves what you are reading.

Habit

Fewer actions, then habit

One line saves the page and names the folder it belongs in. Say it a few times and it becomes muscle memory.

05Use cases

Four ways to use it.

How Scrapyard fits into everyday reading and research.

01

Reading offline

Load up a folder before a flight or a commute, then read it without a connection.

02

Research you can cite

Keep the exact version of an article you read and attach a note to it, so a quote still matches its source.

03

Fragments and PDFs

Clip just the part of a page you need, and keep PDF documents next to the pages that cite them.

04

Assigning TODOs

Mark saved pages as to do, waiting, postponed or done, so the reading list sorts itself and the pages that matter most come first.
